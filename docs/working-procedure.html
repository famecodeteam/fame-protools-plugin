Fame Pro Tools Plugin Working Procedure

Everything the Plugin can do, in plain English - what each button does, where its information comes from, and what it will never do without you clicking a button. It is the Pro Tools counterpart to the Fame Reaper Plugin and the Fame Premiere Plugin: same brain on the server, Pro Tools' hands on the session.

Version: 1.1.0 (8 September 2026). This document is updated in place - the link never changes.

Install: review.fame.so/protools. Needs Pro Tools 2025.10 or newer, any tier. Cubase editors use the same app - see the Cubase chapter at the end.

How the three systems fit together

  • Team Portal - holds each client's rules: cleanup style (how aggressive on fillers, whether to cut repeats, how long a pause is too long), the AM's notes, and who is on the team. It feeds the rules into the Plugin automatically.
  • Fame Pro Tools Plugin - a small app beside Pro Tools. It reads your session through Pro Tools' own scripting service and moves it: jump, cut, silence, level, bounce.
  • Asset Review Tool - where clients listen, comment and approve. Comments flow into the Plugin, and your uploads flow out to it.

You never have to open the Team Portal yourself. If a client's cleanup style looks wrong, tell your PM or AM; they edit it in the Team Portal and the Plugin picks the change up on the next Analyze.

One-time setup

  1. Install the app from review.fame.so/protools and sign in with your review.fame.so email and password.
  2. In Pro Tools, Setup > Preferences > Scripting: make sure scripting is enabled. Open a session; the Plugin's header turns green ("Pro Tools 2025.10 connected"). Press Retry if not.
  3. Save one fade preset named exactly Fame 10ms (select a clip edge, Cmd/Ctrl+F, 10 ms in and out, Save preset). Every cut the Plugin makes gets that fade on its edges. Without the preset the cuts still land and the app tells you fades were skipped.

Loading an episode

Paste any link to the episode - the client review page, the /ve link from the Trello card, or the bare slug - and press Load. No link? Type the client, show or guest name in the search box. Recent episodes sit as chips under the box.

The episode brief shows the show, guest, team, the AM's house notes and this client's watch-outs (mined from their past comments). Shot rules are for video editors and are not shown.

Comments

Every client and QA comment on the episode's assets, audio asset first, newest two versions each. Click a timestamp and Pro Tools jumps the cursor there and plays. Filters: All / Client / QA, and Hide resolved.

A comment marked cuttable is one the server could turn into an exact cut ("delete the um at 3:20"). Its Apply button makes that cut on your session - it refuses rather than guessing, and jumps you there instead when it can't be sure.

Cleanup pass

Analyze audio transcribes the episode's raw recordings from its Drive folder word for word (fillers included) and lists fillers, repeats, dead air, breaths, mouth clicks and "maybe" words. The analysis belongs to the episode: if the video editor already ran it, it appears at once.

Everything is mapped onto your session through the clips that use each recording - by file name, by the Riverside speaker name, or by the speaker's name anywhere in the clip name - so a trimmed, moved or renamed clip still lands right. "522 of 525 found on your timeline" tells you how many mapped; press Refresh after re-arranging.

  • Tick boxes and word chips decide what goes. The client's style pre-ticks the safe defaults; long fillers get a "long" badge.
  • Apply N cuts ripple-cuts each ticked range from every track, back to front, with the fade preset on each seam. Pro Tools' Undo puts each cut back.
  • Silence instead (breaths, clicks, spoken-over fillers) clears the range from the speaker's own track(s) only - a gap, no ripple, session length unchanged. Use it wherever a ripple cut would clip the other person.
  • Spoken over items can only be silenced, never cut - cutting would clip the other speaker's words.
  • Audio issues lists dropouts, clicks and level problems the analysis found. Fix levels next to a speaker-imbalance warning writes the difference as volume automation on that speaker's track(s). It refuses over 30 dB - that is a broken or bleeding mic, not a fader problem. If the track had no volume automation, it assumes the fader sat at 0 dB and says so.
  • Build audio assembly lays out each speaker's raw master on its own named track at 0, then applies the pre-ticked cuts and the level fix. It reads the files from the folder your session already points at, or asks you to pick it; it never downloads from Drive. Missing files are named, never assembled around.

Deliver

  1. Bounce + check does an offline bounce of the main mix to MP3 320 into a "Fame renders" folder beside your session, measures integrated loudness and true peak on your machine, and runs preflight (Fame's -14 LUFS target, true peak, duration, stereo). Already bounced with your own chain? Choose a bounced file gives it the same checks.
  2. Check comments lists open comments that still look unaddressed: cuttable ones are checked mechanically against your session; judgement ones are counted for a last look. Advisory only.
  3. Check music balance measures your speech clips and everything else the same way and warns when the music sits 4 dB or more over the conversation - the "music is too loud" complaint, caught before you bounce.
  4. Upload as vN sends the file straight from your machine to Drive and records it as the audio asset's next version, internal-only, with a what-changed note for the PM listing every action the Plugin took. QA runs on it automatically.

What it will never do without you

  • It never cuts, silences, levels, bounces or uploads until you press the button, and every action shows an in-app confirmation first.
  • It never touches Drive to read media - the raw files come from your disk.
  • It puts your edit mode, link settings, selection and track selection back the way they were after every action.

When something looks wrong

Press Diagnostics at the bottom, then Copy for Tom and paste it to Tom. It contains exactly what Pro Tools answered (version, tracks, clips) - that is what makes a fix quick. The first real sessions will settle a few details the build could not (how Pro Tools reports a trimmed clip's start, the fade preset lookup), so the first week's feedback is the spec.

Cubase chapter

Same app, same brain, same buttons - but Cubase has no scripting door to the arrangement (the MIDI Remote API is for controllers, the project file is binary, a plug-in in the mixer cannot touch clips). So on Cubase the Plugin works through files: you export tracks, it writes them back edited, you import them; jump-to-comment goes over a MIDI cable. Nothing moves in Cubase by itself, and every step in the app says what to do next.

One-time setup (Cubase)

  1. Install from review.fame.so/cubase (the same app), open it and choose Cubase under "Which DAW?" before signing in. It remembers.
  2. Pick the Fame exchange folder in the setup card at the top of the app. Any folder; Cubase exports into it, the Plugin reads from it and writes its own files next to them.
  3. MIDI: in Cubase, Transport > Project Synchronization Setup > Machine Control, tick MMC Slave Active, set MMC Input to Fame Plugin. On Mac that port exists whenever the app is open. On Windows install loopMIDI (free), add one port, pick it in the app's setup card and as Cubase's MMC Input.

Reading your timeline (Cubase)

Select the speaker tracks, File > Export > Selected Tracks, save the .xml into the exchange folder, press Refresh. The cleanup list maps onto that export ("522 of 525 found on your timeline"). Export again after you re-arrange. Keep the speaker tracks on the Linear time base (the clock icon on the track) and dissolve audio parts first - the Plugin refuses both with that exact instruction rather than guessing.

Cuts, silences and Fix levels (Cubase)

Tick and press Apply as usual. The Plugin writes a new archive next to your export (for example DMS27 speakers - Fame cut.xml) and shows the next step: File > Import > Track Archive, pick that file. The new tracks land under your originals with every cut rippled and 10 ms fades on each seam; your originals are the undo - mute or delete them when you are happy. Silence writes a "- Fame silenced" archive (a gap on that speaker's track only). Fix levels writes the difference into the tracks' event volume when the export carries one, otherwise it tells you the exact fader value to set.

Fades: the Plugin copies the shape of a fade Cubase itself wrote. If your export carries no fade at all, put any short fade on one event before exporting and every seam gets a 10 ms fade; otherwise the cut still lands and the app says fades were skipped.

Deliver (Cubase)

Press Export mixdown + check, then in Cubase File > Export > Audio Mixdown (MP3 320 or WAV) into the exchange folder. The app watches the folder, picks the file up when it finishes writing, measures loudness and true peak, runs preflight and offers Upload as vN. Exported somewhere else? Choose an exported file gives it the same checks.

Build audio assembly (Cubase)

Writes one track per speaker referencing the raw files from the folder on your disk, with the pre-ticked cleanup cuts and the level fix already baked into clean copies of the files (ffmpeg, 10 ms fades at every seam, identical cut list on every speaker so sync holds). Import that one archive and you are editing a cleaned 1:1 timeline; the cleanup list unticks itself because those cuts are already in the audio.

While it waits

A file-based flow looks broken while it waits for a file. It is not: every waiting state in the app is a line beginning Next: with the exact Cubase menu to use and a Show file button. If a line ever does not say what to do next, that is a bug - tell Tom.

First week on Cubase

The archive reader was built on real Cubase exports of marker tracks plus an assumed shape for audio events. Your first export is the fixture: if the app says it does not recognise the events, press Diagnostics, Copy for Tom and send it - the fix is a field name, not a rebuild.
